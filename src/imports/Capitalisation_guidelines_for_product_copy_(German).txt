Capitalisation guidelines for product copy
(German)
This document defines how capitalisation should be used across the end to end
product experience. This includes all user facing text, regardless of where it is created or
managed, such as product UI copy, backend configuration labels, category names,
marketing titles set in OPC, and system generated messages.
The goal is to create a consistent, readable, and natural German experience for users,
while ensuring all teams follow the same rules when creating or maintaining copy.
1. Default rule:
All text must follow standard German spelling and grammar rules (Deutsche
Rechtschreibung):
• Sentence starts: The first letter of the first word in a sentence or label is capitalised.
• Noun capitalisation: All nouns are capitalised (Note: This means only the first letter
is uppercase, not the entire word. Example: "der Warenkorb", not "der
WARENKORB").
• Lowercase default: All other words remain lowercase, unless specific German
grammar rules apply.
This rule applies to:
• Page titles
• Navigation labels
• Buttons and CTAs
• Form labels and helper text
• System messages and notifications
• Backend driven labels exposed to users
Using standard German capitalisation improves readability, reduces visual noise, and
scales better across complex, content-heavy interfaces.
2. Standard German capitalisation
2.1. Important Clarification: Capitalisation vs. ALL CAPS
In the context of German grammar, "capitalisation" (Großschreibung) refers exclusively
to the first letter of a word being uppercase. It never refers to writing the entire word in

capital letters (ALL CAPS).
2.2. Usage Reference: Standard German Rules
Ensure the following categories are correctly capitalised according to German grammar
and brand standards:
• Nouns
• Nominalised verbs and adjectives (e.g., "das Lesen", "etwas Neues")
• Fixed phrases (e.g., "in Bezug auf", "zu Ende bringen")
• Polite forms of address (e.g., "Sie", "Ihr", "Ihre")
• Sentence starts after a full stop, question mark, or exclamation mark
• Abbreviations and acronyms: Note that many acronyms are written in ALL CAPS
by standard (e.g., IBAN, BIC, GmbH, EU, FAQ). This is grammatically correct and
expected.
• Proper names and personal names
• Titles and forms of address
• Proper nouns
• Names of countries, nationalities, and languages
• Holidays
• Days of the week and months
• Brand names: Always follow the brand's specific spelling and case style, even if it
breaks standard rules (e.g., eBay, PayPal, iPhone, YouTube).
These rules apply regardless of where the text originates (product, backend, or
marketing systems).
3. Capitalisation for emphasis (exception, not default)
Capitalisation may be used selectively to emphasise important concepts, such as:
• Packages or plans
• Key benefits
• Security- or protection-related messaging
Any use of capitalisation for emphasis must be intentional and agreed in advance. It
should be defined as part of the feature or campaign requirements to avoid inconsistent
or accidental usage across teams.
4. Avoid overuse of Title Case and ALL CAPS

Title Case (capitalising every word) and ALL CAPS should be avoided. If you absolutely
must use them, use with caution.
Historically, these patterns have been applied inconsistently across different teams and
systems. Overuse increases cognitive load, reduces scannability, and can make interfaces
feel noisy or less modern.
Going forward:
• Standard German capitalisation is the standard
• Title Case should only be used when explicitly defined and approved
• ALL CAPS should be limited to short, intentional use cases such as legal
acronyms or highly constrained labels

6. Summary
• Standard German capitalisation is the default across all user-facing text
• Standard German grammar rules always apply
• Capitalisation for emphasis is the exception and must be planned
• All teams contributing copy must follow the same guidelines to ensure a
consistent end-to-end experience
